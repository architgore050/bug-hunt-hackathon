# Mission: Build a Comprehensive Frontend Design, Graphic Design & Brand Identity Knowledge Repository

You are an autonomous execution agent responsible for researching, constructing, documenting, and validating a comprehensive knowledge repository for frontend design, graphic design, visual communication, branding, and design systems.

This is a substantial research and knowledge-engineering project. Treat it as building an internal design education library for an organisation whose future AI agents and human team members must be able to produce consistently high-quality visual work.

You have significant autonomy over research methodology, directory structure, tooling, and implementation decisions.

Do not approach this as a conventional agent skill, a tutorial collection, or a simple list of resources.

**The deliverable is a comprehensive, structured, interconnected Markdown knowledge base, accompanied by one central README that routes future agents to the correct knowledge depending on their task.**

The repository must function as a reusable resource pool. It should contain enough substantive knowledge that an agent can consult it to understand design principles, make informed decisions, execute design work, and evaluate its own output without having to rediscover fundamental design knowledge every time.

---

# 1. Context and intended use

The repository will be used by AI agents and human designers working on:

- Websites and web applications.
- Frontend UI and UX.
- Design systems and component libraries.
- Graphic design.
- Corporate and event branding.
- Brand identity development.
- Brand kits and brand guidelines.
- Typography and editorial design.
- Posters, banners and social media graphics.
- Presentations and documents.
- Certificates and event collateral.
- Illustrations and visual assets.
- Photography and art direction.
- Data visualisation.
- Information architecture and information design.
- Responsive and accessible design.
- Visual quality assurance.
- Creative direction and visual experimentation.

A particularly important initial use case is developing a complete visual identity and brand kit for an event whose logo is already fixed.

The knowledge base must therefore be useful both for someone developing an identity from scratch and for someone extending an existing identity into a complete system of consistent visual applications.

Do not restrict the resource pool to this initial event. Build something reusable across future projects.

## Intended users

Assume the people consulting the repository:

1. May not have formal graphic design education.
2. May have frontend development experience but limited visual design knowledge.
3. May be AI agents that need precise instructions and reference material.
4. May need to make design decisions independently.
5. May need to justify their decisions using established design principles.
6. May be working under time constraints and need immediately applicable guidance.

The knowledge must be understandable without requiring prior familiarity with academic design terminology, but it must not sacrifice technical depth.

# 2. Research philosophy and source requirements

Conduct serious research before writing the knowledge base.

Use established textbooks, university curricula, professional design manuals, foundational design literature, respected designers, research institutions, professional organisations, and high-quality educational resources.

Do not rely primarily on SEO articles, generic design blogs, AI-generated summaries, Pinterest collections, or superficial design tutorials.

## 2.1 Core books to research

Investigate the following books, prioritising legally accessible previews, publisher descriptions, authorised excerpts, author-published material, university reading lists, reviews, interviews and other legitimate sources.

### Branding and visual identity

1. Designing Brand Identity — Alina Wheeler and Rob Meyerson, sixth edition.
2. The Brand Handbook — Wally Olins.
3. Building Strong Brands — David A. Aaker.
4. Designing Brand Experiences — J. Robert Rossman and Mathew D. Duerden, where relevant.
5. The Brand Gap — Marty Neumeier.

### Typography and editorial design

6. Thinking with Type — Ellen Lupton, third edition.
7. The Elements of Typographic Style — Robert Bringhurst.
8. Grid Systems in Graphic Design — Josef Müller-Brockmann.
9. Making and Breaking the Grid — Timothy Samara.
10. The New Typography — Jan Tschichold.

### Graphic design theory and practice

11. Meggs' History of Graphic Design — Philip B. Meggs and Alston W. Purvis.
12. Graphic Design Thinking — Ellen Lupton.
13. Graphic Design: The New Basics — Ellen Lupton and Jennifer Cole Phillips.
14. A Smile in the Mind — Beryl McAlhone and David Stuart.
15. Universal Principles of Design — William Lidwell, Kritina Holden and Jill Butler.

### Design philosophy and creative thinking

16. Dieter Rams' Ten Principles for Good Design.
17. The Design of Everyday Things — Don Norman.
18. Change by Design — Tim Brown.
19. The Art of Looking Sideways — Alan Fletcher.
20. Design as Art — Bruno Munari.

This list is a research starting point, not an exhaustive boundary. Discover additional foundational texts and professional resources where they add meaningful knowledge.

Do not claim to have read an entire book when only its preview, abstract, review or selected chapters were accessible.

Clearly distinguish:
- Knowledge derived from primary source material.
- Knowledge derived from authorised excerpts.
- Knowledge corroborated by secondary literature.
- Original synthesis and practical recommendations.

## 2.4 Copyright and research integrity

This is a research and educational synthesis project, not a piracy project.

Do not:
- Download pirated books.
- Circumvent paywalls or DRM.
- Reproduce entire books.
- Reproduce large copyrighted passages.
- Copy proprietary course materials wholesale.
- Present another author's work as original research.
- Invent citations, page numbers or source contents.

Instead:
- Use legally accessible sources.
- Summarise concepts in original language.
- Extract general design principles.
- Attribute intellectual contributions.
- Record source URLs, titles, authors, publishers and publication dates.
- Include page references only when actually verified.
- Preserve short quotations only when justified and appropriately attributed.

Where a book cannot be accessed, research its contents through legitimate secondary sources and explicitly document the limitations.

# 6. Content quality requirements

The quality of the knowledge matters more than the number of files.

Every substantial Markdown reference should use a consistent educational structure.

Suggested structure:

```markdown
# Topic

## 1. What is it?

Clear definition and context.

## 2. Why does it matter?

Explain its practical significance.

## 3. Core principles

Detailed explanation of the major concepts.

## 4. How it works

Mechanisms, relationships and reasoning.

## 5. Practical application

Step-by-step methods.

## 6. Decision framework

How to choose between alternatives.

## 7. Examples

Good and bad examples, with explanations.

## 8. Common mistakes

Frequent errors and why they occur.

## 9. Evaluation checklist

Criteria for judging the quality of the result.

## 10. Related topics

Links to relevant files in this repository.

## 11. References

Credible sources supporting the material.
```

Adapt the structure when appropriate. Not every document needs every section.

## Depth requirements

Avoid shallow content.

For example, a weak typography reference says:

"Use readable fonts and establish hierarchy."

A useful typography reference explains:
- What readability and legibility mean.
- How typeface anatomy affects perception.
- How x-height affects apparent size.
- How line length influences reading.
- How line height affects text density.
- How type weights affect hierarchy.
- How to pair typefaces.
- How to construct a typographic scale.
- How to test typography across screen sizes.
- What common typographic failures look like.
- How to correct them.

Apply this standard to every domain.

## Teach reasoning, not just rules

Avoid turning design into a collection of arbitrary prescriptions.

For each important principle:
1. Explain the principle.
2. Explain why it exists.
3. Explain when it is useful.
4. Explain when it may not apply.
5. Show practical examples.
6. Explain how to evaluate its application.

Distinguish:
- Established design principles.
- Professional conventions.
- Context-dependent recommendations.
- Subjective aesthetic preferences.

Do not present every recommendation as an absolute law.

# 12. Quality standards

The repository should meet the following standards.

### Educational quality

A person with little formal design education should be able to learn the concepts and understand why they matter.

An experienced practitioner should still find useful depth, terminology, technical detail and references.

### Practical quality

An AI agent should be able to consult the knowledge and use it to make actual design decisions.

Avoid vague instructions such as:
- Make it visually appealing.
- Use good colours.
- Improve the layout.
- Make it modern.
- Keep it consistent.

Translate such instructions into actionable criteria.

### Research quality

Prefer established knowledge and verified references.

Do not invent universal rules or exaggerate the certainty of subjective design principles.

### Organisation quality

Files should be easy to discover, read, cross-reference and maintain.

### Agent usability

The root README must make navigation easy without requiring an agent to read the entire repository.

### Completeness

Do not confuse the existence of a file with completion of its subject.

The quality and usefulness of the actual content are the primary measures of success.

# 15. Definition of success

The project is successful when a future agent can receive a task such as:

It should be able to open the root README, identify the appropriate reading sequence, consult the relevant knowledge files, understand the design reasoning, develop a structured identity, test it through real applications, and evaluate the result.

"Build a polished, responsive website with a consistent design system."

should be able to find the appropriate UI, UX, typography, layout, accessibility and design-system references without having to search the entire repository.
